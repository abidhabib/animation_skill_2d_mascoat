# Packaging playbook (title, thumbnail, hook, retention)

Evidence is thin (a handful of reference thumbnails and one script). Treat these as patterns to TEST, not guarantees.
Measure in YouTube Studio: click-through rate (title + thumbnail) and average view duration (script). Change one thing at a time.

## The genre (works without the words "What If")
Hypothetical premise + a rule that repeats and grows ("every day") + a catch/cost + step-by-step escalation (Day 1 -> Day 30) + the viewer is "you".
Titles: ~70% without "What If", ~30% with it (people search "what if ..."). Keep one consistent series look so the channel is recognisable.

## Title formulas (under ~60 characters; title says the rule, thumbnail shows the result)
number + rule - you are the character - day count - the catch - forbidden/secret - contrast - question without "what if" - bold claim then proof - timeline.
Examples: "You Lose 1 Year of Age Every Day. Day 30 Was Brutal" / "30 Days. 30 Years Older. Here's What Happened" / "Every Lie You Tell Shows as a Number Above Your Head".

## Thumbnail
Hero mascot big bottom-left (~40% of frame), one clear emotion, 2-4 giant words top (white + one yellow word, heavy dark outline), escalation panels (Day 1 / 7 / 15 / 30, hero ages with `age` option), dark character on bright neon gradient, one helper (red arrow, tag, duration badge). Never repeat the title words.

## First 30 seconds
Show the power or the problem BEFORE explaining it. Two quick demonstrations, then one question to the viewer, then the rule in 3 short lines. State the premise by ~0:35, not as the first sentence.

## Retention (the script, not the video editor)
- New twist or new rule every 60-90 s; a cost/twist around 60% of the runtime.
- A question to the viewer every minute or two ("You'd do the same, right?").
- A new visual or camera move at least every ~10 s; cuts on voice pauses (see STYLE_RULES.md).
- Numbers everywhere (Day 21, 3 mm, 160 km/h), one-word punch lines ("Caught.").
- Silence is fine: about 25% of runtime in good reference videos is narration-free, carried by visuals.

## Other characters ("a touch, not a scene")
8 words or fewer per line, only at turning points (a reaction, an accusation, an offer), roughly one every 30-45 s, never a conversation. About 10% of all words at most. Voices: see VOICES.md (4 voices are enough).

## Ending
Pay off the twist, then a sequel hook ("Three days later, a black car stops outside...") and one comment question tied to the story. Subscribe ask only after the payoff.

## Testing plan
3-5 videos with the same structure and different premises. Compare CTR and average view duration. Keep what wins, drop what loses. Re-test titles with Studio "Test & compare" when available.

## Reference set 3: superpower channel (12 videos, 215K to 1M views in ~1 month; measured + seen, 2026-11-19)
Titles (all "What IF You Could / Were X?"): one power + often a number: "10x Faster", "Stop Time", "Shapeshift", "See 10 Seconds Into the Future", "Teleport", "100x Stronger", "Indestructible", "Control Gravity", "Fly", "Shoot Webs", "Copy Powers", "1,000x Faster". 3-7 words, one verb, no subtitle. Lengths 5:28-22:06 (biggest = 1M at 10:19, so runtime is not the limiter). Universal powers (speed, time) beat niche ones in this sample; one channel, one month, so treat as a pattern to test.
Thumbnails (measured): saturation 0.41-0.72 on neon-night backgrounds, but TWO thumbnails are near-white backgrounds (saturation 0.04-0.16, 62-77% bright) so they stand out in a dark feed; contrast sd 53-91; yellow 2-6% of the frame (the text word); dark pixels 31-73% on night scenes.
Rules seen:
1. Thumbnail text is a 2-word reaction or result, ending with a period, NOT the title: "TOO SLOW." "PAUSED." "I ALREADY WON." "TOO MUCH." "NO WINGS." "ONE SHOT." "MINE TOO." "NICE BOAT." First word white, second yellow, bottom-left, heavy dark outline.
2. Show the aftermath or consequence (a crumpled truck, a wrecking ball cracking, a car left behind, a casino dealer stunned), not the power itself.
3. One recurring hero (black round head, half-lidded smug eyes, blue hoodie), dynamic diagonal poses (running, punching, floating, arms crossed).
4. A small second character reacts (shock, laughter) for scale and humour.
5. Number badge top-right for multipliers (10x, 100x, 1,000x) in white with outline.
6. Motion language: speed lines, glow trails, pixel dissolve, neon rim lights; props that show scale (car, truck, boat, wheel).
7. Alternate dark neon-city thumbnails with a clean white one so the channel grid does not look identical.
8. Series consistency: same mascot, palette, text placement, title grammar. This channel proves a uniform "What IF" series can work; the user prefers variety in titles, so keep the STRUCTURE (one power + number, 2-word reaction text) and vary the wording.
Cautions: some of these thumbnails use a real person's face, a branded drink and a famous superhero suit. Do not copy likenesses, brands or other creators' characters; use original equivalents.

## Long-form blueprint: 11-13 minute videos (user's target length, 2026-11-19)
Patterns to TEST, not guarantees. Narration about 1,200-1,500 words (the reference scripts run ~100 words/min including silence); about 22-26 clips of ~30 s each in the project workflow.
| Position | Job | Device |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:40 | Hook | Strong first frame, a concrete moment (no "welcome back"), show the power/problem twice, plant ONE open loop ("by Day 26 you'll cry") |
| ~0:40 | Promise + premise | Say the rule in 3 lines, state what the viewer will get and roughly when |
| 1:00-3:30 | Fun/Setup | Quick wins with a punch word each, numbers on every beat, a viewer question near 2:00, re-hook line before the section ends |
| 3:30-6:00 | Escalation | Bigger stage each step, a side-character touch (<= 8 words) about every 45 s, micro-cliffhanger at the end of each step |
| ~6:00 (50%) | Midpoint twist | The expected rule changes or a cost appears; this is where long videos usually bleed viewers, so give a new reason to stay (a bigger open loop) |
| 6:00-8:30 | Consequences | Stakes up, one more viewer question, pay the SMALL open loops |
| 8:30-10:30 | Climax | Moral choice with a countdown, pay the title's promise (still before the very end) |
| 10:30-12:00 | Payoff + new loop | Emotional payoff, reverse twist, sequel hook, comment question; subscribe ask only after payoff |
Re-hook every 90-120 s: a teaser line ("but that was not the worst part"), a question to the viewer, a new Day/number card, or a visual/audio change.
Pattern interrupts: new location or camera move about every 10 s, cut on voice pauses, a music/sound change at each section break, one 3-5 s silence beat before the big moments, vary narration speed.
Lengthen without padding: add more tests, more places, branching consequences, a second character's mini-story; never repeat the same joke type more than three times.
Packaging triad: title, thumbnail and first 30 s must make the same promise; deliver it (misleading packaging may raise clicks but hurts trust and retention). After publishing: read the retention graph, find the steepest drops, shorten or re-hook those parts next time.

## User's retention rules + pre-record checklist (added 2026-11-19; judged sound, wording about "triggering the algorithm" is not verifiable)
Rules: (1) "Therefore / But" chain - connect beats with consequence or complication, never "and then". (2) Re-hook every ~90 s (card, audio shift, location change, teaser line). (3) Universal stakes - tie every twist to time, money, family, love, survival or status (test others too). (4) Silence before impact - drop music 1-2 s before the biggest line. (5) Kill filler with branching tests - add a test, a person affected or a harder consequence instead of slow narration.
Checklist before recording (aim 5/5; it improves odds, it does not guarantee views): [ ] one-second clarity (a 12-year-old and a 50-year-old can state the premise from title + first 15 s) [ ] an open loop active at every minute mark [ ] at least two distinct emotions (e.g. excitement -> dread -> warmth) [ ] one share-worthy line/moment [ ] an ending question with no single right answer.
